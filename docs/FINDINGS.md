# Findings log (user's own observations, verbatim intent)

## Steps 1–2: Scout + evidence (Rent)

1. **Photos show surroundings, not the unit.** In the first listings reviewed, few images show the actual room or building amenities; most show surrounding scenery/skyline to look luxurious or show location. The searcher can't see what they'd actually live in.
2. **Recycled / stock unit photos.** Same apartment photos appear across many listings; when contacted, agents send different videos/photos of the real unit. Listings don't always disclose that photos are indicative. (Listing #2)
3. **Missing trust signals.** Building name not stated in listing; agent has no ratings → hard to trust. (Listing #2)
4. **Same "area" photos reused across different buildings.** Surrounding-area images in the last 2–3 listings look identical, as if pulled from Google, and don't match the feel of the actual units. (Listings #1–3)
5. **Photos can't prove location.** Even plausible unit photos don't show the view from window/balcony, so the searcher can't confirm the unit is in the claimed area. (Listing #3)
6. **Agents without ratings recur.** (Listings #2, #3)
7. **True monthly cost not stated.** Description doesn't say DEWA, chiller, tourism tax, VAT or other fees are included/excluded. Searcher must call the agent just to learn if it fits budget, and may only find out it doesn't after the call. Wants this listed upfront, or at least flagged as "ask the agent about this". (Listing #4)
8. **Price doesn't match what you get.** A plain-looking Marina studio is priced the same as a modern Business Bay one; more luxurious options exist at the same price. Searcher can't tell what justifies the price. (Listing #5)
9. **Floor and view not stated.** Matters for a searcher who wants a high floor with a view. (Listing #5)
10. **Inclusions stated = trust boost.** Listing #5 lists what is included/excluded, which the user called a good touch. (Listing #5)
11. **Same "bait" photo set across many agents.** One set of DAMAC Maison Privé interior photos appears on multiple listings (#2, #6, and the next). From past experience, ~95% of listings using these photos sent a different video/unit when contacted, and that look was never found in Business Bay. (Listing #6)
12. **Duplicate listing of the same unit.** #7 is the same unit as #6 (same price, size and photos) posted by a different agent (same agency, JSR Global). The searcher sees two "options" that are really one. (Listings #6, #7)
13. **Building/amenity photos add realism.** #7 adds corridor and amenity shots, making it feel more real than #6. (Listing #7)
14. **Generic community photos misrepresent the micro-location.** The same Business Bay community images appear on every listing; but Business Bay is large, and not every part has that view or is close to Downtown. Fine as "area flavour", misleading as "your view". (Listings #1–8)
15. **"Studio" that is really a hotel room.** #8 shows just a bed, no kitchen, yet it's listed as a studio at a high price (AED 6,499 for 334 sqft, the highest AED/sqft so far). For someone who cooks, the missing kitchen is a deal-breaker that the listing type hides. (Listing #8)
16. **Clear lease type builds confidence.** #9 says plainly that it's short-term only (daily/weekly/monthly, not annual). For a visitor this is exactly the signal needed, and it wasn't visible on earlier listings. (Listing #9)
17. **Disclosed fees build trust, but reveal the real price.** #9 lists tourism tax, VAT, service charge and a checkout/cleaning fee. The user liked it most, but once fees are added it's even further above budget. (Listing #9)
18. **What made the first "liked" listing:** enough real room photos + real studio with kitchen + good amenities + agent rated >4 + clear lease type + fees stated. (Listing #9)
19. **Monthly/flexible terms = fit for a visitor.** #10's monthly basis lets her stay 3 months and extend 1–2 more. A strong reason to like it. (Listing #10)
20. **"Additional fees apply" with no amounts forces a call.** The fees are named but not priced, so she can't tell if it fits her budget without contacting the agent. (Listing #10)
21. **Community photos actively mislead on far-off buildings.** User visited #11 in person: it sits at the extreme edge of Business Bay, overlooking highways, and the places shown in its community photos need long travel. Someone who knows the area catches this; a first-timer wouldn't. (Listing #11, first-hand)
22. **Transit time in minutes is valued.** Some listings give metro/bus-stop distance in minutes (e.g. #10: DMCC metro ~15 min walk), which is useful. #11 omits it, and the user knows it's far. Absence of transit info can itself be a signal. (Listings #10, #11)
23. **The price changes between search card, detail page and description.** #12: search card AED 7,500/mo, then the detail page says 95,000/yr, then the description says 7,500 applies only to a 6-month booking. Three different "prices" for one listing. (Listing #12; similar in #11)
24. **Headline price is conditional on a commitment the searcher can't make.** The best rate is tied to a fixed term (6 months), but a visitor with an uncertain stay can't commit, so the advertised price isn't her price. (Listings #11, #12)
25. **Same building, very different units.** #10 and #12 are both Studio One, but the units look very different, so building photos say little about the unit. (Listings #10, #12)
26. **Cover photo isn't the unit.** #13's search card opens on a hotel reception image; she had to click in to see the actual room. (Listing #13)
27. **Same images, different prices.** #13's photos were seen on another, cheaper listing. (Listing #13)
28. **Long-stay offers are buried in descriptions.** #13 is the first listing with long-stay special offers, and she only found them by reading the description. She'd like to see offers without opening every listing. (Listing #13)
29. **Wants a cost calculator.** Descriptions list 5% this, 7% that; she wants to calculate the real total without doing the maths herself. (Listing #13)
30. **Multi-month discounts exist but aren't priced.** #14 mentions special rates for 2–12 months but doesn't say how much, so she'd contact the agent to learn the 3-month price. (Listing #14)
31. **She grades listings A/B.** At the same price she expects more luxury; #15 is "B": acceptable fallback, not first choice. A good agent rating can push her to inquire despite a thin description. (Listing #15)
32. **Rating count matters as much as the score.** 3.8 from 104 ratings feels more trustworthy than 4.5 from 2. (Listing #16 vs #1/#2)
33. **"All bills incl. (DEWA, chiller)" removes budget anxiety.** (Listing #16)
34. **Negotiation hooks hide in descriptions.** "Discounted for long-term" is a lever to get 7,650 → 7,500, but only if she remembers it when chatting. (Listings #14, #16)
35. **🔑 Losing track after ~15 listings.** Her mind gets foggy about which she liked, why, and what each description said. (Listing #16)
36. **🔑 The conversation happens off Property Finder.** From India: agents ignore Indian numbers (suspect scams); WhatsApp calls are blocked in the UAE, so it's text only. With ~10 agents on WhatsApp she can't tell which property or agent is which or what was discussed.
37. **🔑 Her workaround is an Excel tracker** (link, agent, advantages/hooks, what to discuss, past discussion), but it's outside PF and clunky on a phone on the go. Mid-chat she must reopen the PF link, re-read the description, and often forgets the hook.
38. **What she wishes existed:** per-listing memory of *why she picked it*, *what to discuss*, and *the hook for her case*, available while talking to each agent.
39. **Distances in the description are a plus.** #17 lists walk/drive times to metro and landmarks (DMCC metro 6–8 min walk, Marina Mall 8–10, JBR 10–12, Downtown 20–22 min drive). Exactly what a newcomer who doesn't know the city needs. (Listing #17)
40. **One agency, same template, many units.** Colife's #16 and #17 share the description wording ("discounted for long-term", bills included) and the same building with different agents. Useful for comparing offers, confusing for tracking. (Listings #16, #17)
41. **Looks drive the "like".** She liked #18 mainly for how the apartment looks. Visual taste (luxury, view) is a primary filter that no search filter captures. (Listing #18)
42. **🔑 Her agent-trust rule:** many reviews + rating just under 4 (3.8–3.9) = trust. Few reviews (e.g. 4) + rating under 4 = apprehensive. Volume of reviews beats the score. (Listing #20; see also #32)
43. **Fallback when unsure: ask for real videos.** After many listings, when photos are ambiguous (room vs area mismatch, room looks small), she'd simply ask the agent for actual videos of the unit. (Listing #20)
44. **Photo size perception can mislead.** #20 looked small in photos, yet it's 686 sqft, the largest studio so far. Photos alone don't convey size. (Listing #20)
